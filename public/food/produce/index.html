Kitchen measures

Produce

Pieces, heaps, bunches and naira-worth, in grams.

Recipes ask for grams, but at home and in the market food comes as pieces, cups, heaps and bunches. Here is what we know, and how to find out the rest.

Weigh your own heap or bunch (5 minutes)
  1. Buy the usual heap or bunch. Pick three of the same kind.
  2. Ask the seller to weigh each one on their scale, or weigh them at home.
  3. Write down the middle weight.
  4. Next time, use that number. Change it when the size changes.

For a heap, count the pieces and multiply: 5 medium tomatoes × 125 g is about 625 g. Heaps differ from seller to seller, so ask for one to be weighed once. Nigerian tomatoes and tatashe vary a lot with type and season.

Leafy vegetables

Leaves are tied in bunches, and a bunch is whatever the seller ties, so there is no standard weight and we found no reliable figure. For cooking, measure by the cup instead.

By the cup: 1 cup of raw pumpkin leaves weighs about 39 g, and 1 cup of chopped sweet potato leaves about 35 g (Textbook figure (US)). Ugu is a different pumpkin, so treat this as a guide. To find a bunch's weight, weigh three bunches and use the middle one.

Groundnuts, nuts and seeds

By the tin: use the Units page. A big derica of shelled groundnuts is about 570 g, and in the shell about 260 g.

By the cup: 1 US measuring cup (240 ml) of shelled groundnuts weighs about 146 g (Textbook figure (US)).

By the kernel: about 32 shelled kernels weigh 28 g (1 ounce), so one kernel is about 0.9 g. Nigerian groundnuts are often smaller, so count and weigh a few. A pod in the shell weighs about 1½ g (Study, three Nigerian varieties), so counting pods is slow: use the tin.

No figure found yet, weigh yours: okra, garden egg, shelled egusi, ogbono, tigernut, locust bean (iru).

Oils and water

1 litre of water weighs 1 kg. 1 litre of palm oil weighs a little under 1 kg, about 0.9 kg (Unchecked). Palm-oil can sizes are on the Units page.

How sure are we?

  • National survey a weight printed in the national household survey's unit list. Not weighed for this page.
  • Study measured in a published study.
  • Textbook figure from a reference table, often for US varieties. Nigerian produce may differ.
  • Unchecked a rough figure we could not confirm.

Everything here is desk research. None of it has been weighed by the author yet, so use "Weigh your own heap or bunch" before you rely on it.
