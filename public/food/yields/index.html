Kitchen measures

Yields

What you get after peeling, cooking or measuring heaped.

A recipe may ask for peeled, cooked or level amounts, but you buy the whole thing raw. These numbers help you buy the right amount.

After peeling

Buy = what the recipe needs ÷ the share left. Example: a recipe needs 1 kg of peeled yam. 1 ÷ 0.84 = about 1.2 kg to buy.

The tuber figures are averages from an FAO report on African staples. Thick peeling loses more. The plantain figure is the flesh share of the finger, from a study of five plantain types grown in Benin. Leaves such as ugu lose their stems: no figure found yet.

After cooking

FoodCooked weighs aboutExampleHow sure
Beans (cowpea)3 times the dry weight1 kg dry makes about 3 kg cookedWorked out
Rice2½ to 3 times the dry weight1 cup dry makes about 3 cups cookedUnchecked

Beans: boiled cowpeas are about 70% water in the US food tables, and dry beans hold about 12% water (our assumption). Boil them longer or softer and they weigh more. Rice: cooking sites agree on about 3 cups cooked from 1 cup dry. Parboiled and local rice may differ, so cook a small amount once and note what you get.

Level or heaped

Always measure level: scrape the top flat. A heaped container can hold a quarter to a third more (Unchecked), which spoils any table on these pages.

How sure are we?

  • Study measured in a published study.
  • Textbook figure from a report or reference table, not measured on Nigerian food for this page.
  • Worked out calculated from other figures, with the assumption stated.
  • Unchecked a rough figure we could not confirm.

Everything here is desk research. None of it has been weighed by the author yet, so cook a small amount and check.
