Kitchen measures

Units

Tins, dericas, kongos and bags, in kilograms.

Tins and dericas are not exact. Check your own once (below), then trust the numbers for your own tins.

Check your own tin (10 minutes)
  1. Get 1 kg of rice that is already weighed: a sealed 1 kg pack, or ask the seller to weigh 1 kg for you.
  2. Fill your tin with rice. Scrape the top flat with a ruler or knife, then pour it into a bowl.
  3. Repeat until the 1 kg is finished. Count the tins, for example "6 tins and a little more".
  4. Write that number on the tin with a marker. Do the same for each container you use.

No weighed rice and no scale? Use the water test further down.

A 50 kg bag of rice holds about 64 to 71 big dericas. Names change from town to town, so ask the seller how many litres the container holds. A bigger 410 g milk tin also exists: no reliable weight found yet, so weigh yours. Always measure level, because a heaped container can hold a quarter to a third more (Unchecked).

Buying by naira

Many people buy by money, not by container: "₦500 of garri". The weight then depends on today's price. Work it out like this: your money ÷ the price of one derica = how many dericas you get. Then use the table above.

Example with made-up prices. A derica of rice costs ₦1,500. ₦500 ÷ ₦1,500 = ⅓ derica. A big derica is about ¾ kg, so you get about 250 g.

No weighed rice? Use water

1 ml of water weighs 1 g. Fill your tin with water and measure it in a measuring jug. Other foods weigh a fraction of that.

Recipes: cup or tin?

In recipes, "a cup" may mean a 250 ml measuring cup (many popular Nigerian food blogs use this) or the milk tin. They are not the same size, so check which one your recipe means.

How sure are we?
