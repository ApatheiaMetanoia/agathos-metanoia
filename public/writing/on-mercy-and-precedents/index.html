My first writing here should be the one of the first writing/opinion piece (of two only) I personally published anywhere (It was on Medium — now deleted). The piece is almost 2 years old (roughly a month after Trump’s second term), and as such, carries features which may seem outdated — but I reckon its central message still rings true. Thus, introducing:

On Mercy and Precedents

You are a female in Nigeria.

Imagine I go out, maybe to a stadium with you and your man. You hold your man’s hand and kiss him — there might even be some “Awwwnnnn.”

Then, I, who supposedly owns a penis, see a man, a friend, a lover, perhaps. I hold his hand and kiss him — in public, in the view of everyone, in Nigeria. Imagine the stares I’ll get. Not lovely stares, I assure you; hostile stares.

Not only hostile stares — some “well-meaning” citizens would tap me and my man harshly and ask us why we were doing what we were doing. Perhaps, asking is a stretch, a misguided and hopeful stretch. It’s more like they’ll slap our heads and be aggressive with us. And if law enforcement agencies are around, they may not restrain our aggressors. No, they’ll more likely join in the “righteous aggressiveness” towards us.

This is a hopeful scenario. We are in a stadium, so there might be some civility.

If we were walking outside together, those “well-meaning citizens” would have beaten us into a stupor, publicly — to “drive out the gay” in us.

A substantial number of Christians (any religion, really), whom Jesus (and their respective spiritual leaders) pleaded to love their neighbours and be compassionate, would applaud such “discipline”. “It’s the lord’s work,” they may say. This 2026 Pew survey is a damning read for Nigeria.

If I wear a gown outside, the same will happen — maybe worse, even. And if I report to law enforcement, I may be thrown in prison — “for my good” — to remove the madness of dressing and behaving “unacceptably and unnaturally”. As though what I put on affects others’ lives.

But, Nigeria is a few years backward, and I’ve accepted such — while conversations with a few folks have me hoping more progressive years are nigh. The USA was once my progressive hope; not my favourite hope, but my strongest and most influential hope. Do you know that they did withhold aid and trade from countries with terrible LGBTQIA2S+ records?

Perhaps, sanctions rarely work. But, even with Jonathan’s terrible Same Sex Marriage (Prohibition) Act, Nigeria rarely, if ever, formally convicts LGBTQIA2S+ folks — a lot of harassments, assaults, and extortions though; the Sharia states are also a different beast — and queer celebrities are tolerated (provided they remain in a box).

Well, America is becoming hopeless. Not half as bad as Nigeria, to be clear. They may not publicly beat or imprison queer folks. Not yet. But, we can see how they are slowly eroding the little rights they’ve won over many hard years. Trump’s manifesto features a lot of hate against minorities, and he’s been enforcing it — with immigrants, diversity programs, and queer folks. For example: Government-issued IDs now only reflect the gender binary.

Martin Niemöller’s poem — “First they came” — is about precedents. They take the first minority’s rights, and you don’t care, or you cheer for it because of religion and tradition. They take the second minority’s rights, and you don’t care because it doesn’t concern you. Then they take your rights because a precedent has been set.

The thing is that the US has so much influence — economically, militarily, allies — so if they are eroding some rights, other countries may be emboldened to erode more rights. This is why this piece heavily references the USA. To add a sour gloss to this point, US evangelical campaigners have also been documented promoting anti-LGBT legislation in Africa.

Whatever happens, dear reader, this is addressed to you with love and hope. Wherever and whatever country you are in now, wherever and whatever situation you may be in a few years from now; you’ll likely have a lot of legal might — whether as a voter, protester, lawyer, well-meaning citizen, etcetera. I hope you’ll have compassion and empathy for those different from you.

Just as Bishop Mariann Edgar Budde begged Trump to have mercy, and understanding, on the minorities, I’m begging you the same too.

Have mercy and try to make sure that everyone is free to do as they please in their personal life — away from religious differences and traditions, away from whatever you and society think is right for their personhood. Try to legally fight for those who society wants to police their body, their being, their personality — even if they are non-human.

I’ll still keep hoping that you, dear reader, will be more open and accepting regarding these issues. Perhaps, you may still be disgusted by these things, but due to your empathy, you’ll accept us for who we are.
